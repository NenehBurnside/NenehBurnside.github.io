University of Maryland Housing Crisis

Rising enrollment pushed students into a competitive off-campus market

By: Neneh Burnside

Source: WDG Architecture

COLLEGE PARK, Md.-Rising enrollment at the University of Maryland tighted housing availability for the 2026-27 academic year, pushing returning students onto waitlists and forcing them to search for costly off-campus hosuing alternatives.

University officials notified students that limited space and high demand forced the Department of Resident Life to place some applicants on a housing waitlist.

More than 40,000 students enrolled at UMD including about 30,000 undergraduates, highlighting a growing gap between struden population and available housing, according to the Campus Counts reports and the university's Common Data Set

The university failed to expand housing capacity fast enough to keep pace with that growth. UMD offers 9,601 beds across 39 residence halls, short of accommodating the university’s large undergraduate population, according to the Department of Resident Life.

Figure 1. On-campus housing capacity compared to student enrollment at UMD.

Only a fraction of the student body who do not commute can be housed on campus, leaving thousands of students to compete for limited space to turn to off-campus housing options.

While first-year students have been guaranteed housing if deadlines were not met, upperclassmen have been placed on waitlists, limiting the ability to secure stable housing for the upcoming year.

Students placed on the waitlist are not eligible to participate in room selection and must wait until May for updates on housing status.

Source: Neneh Burnside

“I think the housing process is unfair because they prioritize newer students than existing students,which really in my opinion doesn't make sense monetarily,”

-Josephine Simmons, a current UMD student.

“If I had not gotten off-campus housing next year, I’m currently on the waitlist as a rising junior, which does not make sense to me.” said Simmons.

As on-campus housing have been filled, students are pushed into the off-campus housing market, where prices have steadily increased. In College Park, median rent prices have risen in recent years, with typical monthly rents for apartments often exceeding $2,000, according to Rental Market Trends.

Figure 2. Growth of rent prices in College Park, Maryland. Source: Zillow Rental Market Trends

Rent prices in College Park have risen steadily over time, with recent averages exceeding $2,300 per month, placing additional financial strain on students forced into the off-campus market.

Median household income in College Park remains limited compared to rising housing costs, underscoring affordability challenged students without full-time employment and added financial pressure for students already struggling to find available units, according to Census data.

Rental demand near campus has also increased as more students compete for a limited number of apartments and houses, leading to shared living arrangements or signing leases months in advance, according to Zillow Rental Market Trends.

UMD said high demand is in part by large incoming classes expected in Fall 2026. Enrollment grew consistently in recent years, steady increases in both applications and admitted students, further straining campus resources like housing, according to Campus Counts.

Students affected by the shortage have been encouraged to explore off-campus housing options while final decisions are processed.

Students reported signing leases without knowing where they will secure on-campus housing. Pressure to secure backup housing in a competitive rental market, where availability can change quickly and prices continue to climb.

University officials said they expect to provide updates on housing availability by May 15th.
